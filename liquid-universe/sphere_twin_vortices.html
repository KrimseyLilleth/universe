Sphere: Twin Vortices

from the drawing · live simulation

Two counter-spinning vortices sit on a tilted axis inside a sphere. A cage of field elements (the ticks) presses inward and holds everything in.

The void is not drawn. The two vortices face each other with the same polarity, so their fields cancel exactly along the middle. The simulation measures where the field falls near zero and marks it. Nothing holds a particle there.

Gold — strongly held particles
Cyan — only just held
Red — inside the void, flying free

Counter-spin is what holds it open. The two vortices twist opposite ways, so the twist cancels to zero in the middle (see net twist). Switch it off and the void collapses to about a tenth of its width — the balance is doing real work.

β compares how hard the plasma pushes out against how hard the cage pushes in. It is shown relative to the balanced default, so β near 1 is the balance point; well above 1 and the cage is losing.

Particles move by the real Lorentz force (Boris integrator). They are not following a path I wrote for them — they spiral, mirror off the cage and drift straight through the void because that is what the field does to them.
