The law

derived from your three conditions

Write α for the angle the streamline makes with the toroidal direction, and θ for how far round the tube you are (0° at the outer rim, 180° at the inner rim). One function hits all three of your boundary values, and only one.

Pitch angle
tan α=φ  cot(θ / 2)

At θ = 0 the cotangent is infinite, so α = 90°. At θ = 180° it is zero, so α = 0. At θ = 90° it is exactly 1, so tan α = φ — your crown condition, with nothing left over to tune.

Rate of turning
|dα/dθ|= 1 √5 + cos θ

The golden ratio vanishes into a bare √5. That happens for one value of the pitch constant only: k = φ is the unique k where the cos θ term arrives with coefficient exactly 1, because that is what k² − k − 1 = 0 says.

Where the model bites

read before you trust it

Four things the picture does that your description probably did not intend, or did not yet say.

Never returns
“Back around again” does not happen toroidally. Making the inner rim exactly 100% toroidal turns it into a closed loop of the flow, and no streamline can cross a closed loop. Each line is born infinitesimally close to the inner rim, runs once over the crown, across the outer rim and back — a complete poloidal circuit — but takes infinitely many toroidal laps to leave and to land. The saving grace is that the divergence is logarithmic and therefore gentle: the gap to the inner rim shrinks by a factor of e every 2 radians of toroidal travel, so about 23× per lap. One and a half laps already puts you within a degree.
Your choice
Which way the flow drifts below the equator. The pitch angle is symmetric top-to-bottom, but the flow direction is not determined by it. Either the toroidal drift keeps the same sense all the way round (the default here), or it reverses across the outer rim, putting a shear layer in the equatorial plane. Both satisfy everything you specified. The checkbox switches them.
Not an accident
arctan φ at the crown may be forced rather than assumed. You gave it as a condition. But on a Klimax torus the crown sits at height r above the equatorial plane and distance R from the axis, and R/r = φ — so arctan(R/r) is arctan φ already. If the general law is tan α = (R/r) cot(θ/2), your crown angle is a consequence of the aspect ratio, not an extra input. Drag the aspect slider and watch the crown pitch follow it.
Don’t overclaim
Some of the pretty numbers are not about φ at all. The circuit average of ½, and the fact that the fastest and slowest turning rates multiply to ¼, hold for any pitch constant k — they follow from α running 90° down to 0° over half a turn and nothing else. What is genuinely φ-specific: the cos θ coefficient being exactly 1, the extremes being 1/2φ and φ/2, their difference being exactly ½, and their ratio being φ². Move the k slider and watch which numbers stay put.
Direction only
This is a direction field, not yet a fluid. It says which way the flow points everywhere, and nothing about how fast. To make it an actual incompressible flow you would have to solve for a speed that keeps the divergence zero on the surface — and near the inner rim, where the lines crowd together without limit, that speed has to do something drastic. Worth knowing before the picture gets attached to any conserved quantity.
